\begin{abstract}
Proteina is a flow-based generative model for protein backbone structure that achieves state-of-the-art quality without explicit geometric constraints in its architecture --- geometry enters only as an additive pair bias to attention logits.
We study how this geometric inductive bias interacts with learned content representations during the denoising trajectory, using three complementary analytical lenses: a pre-softmax signal decomposition, a post-softmax functional evaluation, and causal ablation experiments.
Analysing both a 60M and a 200M parameter model, we find that (i)~the pair bias alone predicts protein contacts $5\times$ better than the content score at both scales, (ii)~the geometric bias is dispensable during early generation but essential during late structural refinement --- a causal finding that replicates identically across model sizes, and (iii)~despite this causal equivalence, the descriptive geometry--content balance is strongly scale-dependent: the 60M model's Layer~0 shows $8\times$ bias dominance while the 200M model is near-balanced ($R \approx 0.6$).
This dissociation between descriptive signal magnitude and causal necessity highlights the importance of multi-lens interpretability, and demonstrates that geometric inductive biases play functionally irreplaceable roles that scale with model capacity in distinct ways.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Generative models for protein backbone structure have advanced rapidly, with flow matching emerging as a leading paradigm~\citep{lipman2023flow}.
Proteina~\citep{geffner2025proteina}, an ICLR 2025 Oral paper, scales flow-based protein generation to state-of-the-art quality by combining a transformer backbone with \emph{Pair Bias Attention} (PBA) --- a mechanism that fuses learned content scores with geometric pair biases encoding inter-residue distances and sequence separation.

Proteina is notable in the context of geometric deep learning because it achieves strong performance \emph{without} explicit geometric constraints in its architecture.
Unlike SE(3)-equivariant networks~\citep{thomas2018tensor,fuchs2020se3} or invariant point attention~\citep{jumper2021alphafold2}, Proteina's transformer layers are standard (non-equivariant) attention and MLP blocks.
Geometry enters only through the pair bias $B$, an additive term to the pre-softmax attention logits derived from pairwise distances and sequence separation.
Crucially, because $B$ is computed from SE(3)-invariant inputs (pairwise distances), it provides a \emph{soft invariant prior} --- encoding geometric information without constraining the network's outputs to be equivariant or invariant, in contrast to architectures that enforce such symmetries by construction.
This reflects a broader trend in geometric deep learning: moving from hard-coded architectural symmetries toward soft, representation-based geometric priors --- as seen in vision (ViT~\citep{dosovitskiy2021image} replacing CNNs), graph learning (TokenGT~\citep{kim2022tokengt} replacing message passing), and protein structure prediction (AlphaFold3~\citep{abramson2024alphafold3} replacing equivariant modules with diffusion).

This raises a natural question: \textbf{in a model where geometry enters only as an additive bias to attention logits, how much work does this bias actually do --- and when?}
During inference, Proteina integrates a velocity field from pure noise ($t{=}0$) to a structured protein backbone ($t{=}1$) via ${\sim}100$ Euler steps.
At each step, every attention layer computes:
\begin{equation}
    A = \mathrm{softmax}(C + B)
    \label{eq:attention}
\end{equation}
where $C = QK^\top / \sqrt{d}$ is the content score derived from sequence representations, and $B$ is the pair bias projected from distance and sequence-separation features.
The additive decomposition in Equation~\ref{eq:attention} invites a systematic study of how each component shapes attention --- and ultimately structure --- across the generation trajectory.

However, decomposing contributions through a nonlinear function (softmax) has no single correct solution.
We therefore ask three complementary questions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Descriptive:} How much pre-softmax signal does each component carry, and how does this balance evolve during generation?
    \item \textbf{Functional:} Which component produces more structurally useful attention patterns?
    \item \textbf{Causal:} When and where is the geometric bias necessary for generating valid structures?
\end{enumerate}
Each question motivates a distinct analytical lens --- pre-softmax signal decomposition, post-softmax contact prediction, and causal ablation --- which together provide a triangulated view of the geometric bias's role.
We apply all three lenses to both a 60M and a 200M parameter model to test whether findings generalise across scale.
Section~\ref{sec:methods} describes the models and metrics; Section~\ref{sec:results} presents results; Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses implications for geometric deep learning.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Models and Generation}

We analyse three Proteina model variants to test scale and architecture dependence:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{60M} (v1.3): 10 layers, 8 heads, $d_\text{token}{=}512$, $d_\text{pair}{=}256$, no triangle updates.
    \item \textbf{200M no-tri} (v1.2): 15 layers, 12 heads, $d_\text{token}{=}768$, $d_\text{pair}{=}256$, no triangle updates.
    \item \textbf{200M tri} (v1.1): 15 layers, 12 heads, $d_\text{token}{=}768$, $d_\text{pair}{=}512$, with triangle multiplicative updates to the pair representation.
\end{itemize}
The first two models share the same architecture at different scales; the third adds a second geometric inductive bias (triangle updates that iteratively refine the pair representation).
All models include 10 register tokens and use the pair bias attention mechanism (Equation~\ref{eq:attention}).
Proteins of length $n{=}100$ are generated unconditionally via SDE sampling with self-conditioning.
Attention logit matrices $C$ and $B$ are captured every 5th timestep, yielding 20 temporal snapshots per generation.
The 60M experiments use 5 random seeds; the 200M experiments use 3. Error bars report $\pm 1$ standard deviation.

\subsection{Three Analytical Lenses}

\paragraph{Lens 1: Pre-softmax signal decomposition.}
We define the \emph{logit dominance ratio} $R = \|B\|_F / \|C\|_F$, measuring the relative Frobenius-norm magnitude of each component.
$R \gg 1$ indicates geometry-dominated attention; $R \approx 1$ indicates balance.
Since softmax is invariant to row-constant shifts ($\mathrm{softmax}(\mathbf{x} + c) = \mathrm{softmax}(\mathbf{x})$), we also compute a \emph{row-centred} variant $R_c = \|\tilde{B}\|_F / \|\tilde{C}\|_F$, where $\tilde{B}$ and $\tilde{C}$ are obtained by subtracting each row's mean.
$R_c$ captures only within-row variance --- the component that actually shapes the attention distribution.
We additionally track attention entropy $\hat{H} = H / \log n \in [0,1]$ and spatial alignment $\rho$ (Pearson correlation between attention weights and inverse ground-truth distances).

\paragraph{Lens 2: Post-softmax functional evaluation.}
We evaluate how well attention maps predict true residue contacts.
For each timestep and layer, we compute Contact Precision@$L/5$ using three attention variants: $\mathrm{softmax}(C + B)$ (full), $\mathrm{softmax}(B)$ (bias-only), and $\mathrm{softmax}(C)$ (content-only).
Ground truth contacts are defined from the model's own final generated structure (C$\alpha$ distance $< 8$\,\AA, $|i{-}j| \geq 6$), following~\citet{rao2020transformer}.

\paragraph{Lens 3: Causal ablation.}
We zero out the pair bias $B$ under six conditions: (i)~\emph{full ablation} ($B{=}0$ at all timesteps), (ii)~\emph{early-only B} ($B$ active for $t \leq 0.5$, then zeroed), (iii)~\emph{late-only B} ($B{=}0$ for $t \leq 0.5$, then active), (iv)~\emph{layer-0 ablation} ($B{=}0$ in the first layer only), (v)~\emph{deep ablation} ($B{=}0$ in layers 5--9 for 60M, layers 8--14 for 200M), and (vi)~\emph{baseline} (unmodified).
Structural quality is assessed via the metrics defined in Table~\ref{tab:metrics} (3 seeds per condition).

\paragraph{Supporting analyses.}
We additionally perform a \emph{structure lens} analysis, applying the model's coordinate decoder to intermediate layer representations to measure how structure emerges across layers~\citep{nostalgebraist2020logitlens}.
We also analyse register token attention absorption across layers and sequence-separation decomposition of the logit dominance ratio.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Descriptive: The Geometry--Content Balance}

Figures~\ref{fig:trajectory} and~\ref{fig:trajectory200m} show the three core metrics across the denoising trajectory for both models.

\textbf{Layer 0 as geometric signal processor: a scale-dependent effect.}
In the 60M model, Layer~0 at $t{=}0$ shows logit dominance $R = 7.93 \pm 0.07$ --- the geometric bias is nearly $8\times$ louder than the content score.
After an initial dip to $R \approx 3$ around $t{=}0.2$, dominance rises again to $R \approx 5.7$ by $t{=}1$.
Deeper layers maintain $R \approx 1$ throughout.

The 200M model presents a strikingly different picture (Figure~\ref{fig:trajectory200m}): Layer~0 at $t{=}0$ shows $R \approx 0.57$ --- the content score is actually \emph{louder} than the geometric bias, the opposite of the 60M pattern.
All layers start near-balanced ($R \approx 0.5$--$0.7$) and rise modestly during generation, with $R$ reaching $\sim$1.0--1.5 by $t{=}1$.
The ``geometric interpreter'' phenomenon --- a single layer dominated by $B$ --- appears to be a property of smaller models, not a universal feature of this architecture.

\textbf{Entropy crystallises in a wave.}
In the 60M model, all layers start near-uniform ($\hat{H} \approx 0.85$).
Layer~0 crystallises first, with normalised entropy dropping sharply; deeper layers follow progressively later --- a wave-like propagation consistent with bottom-up structure formation.
The 200M model shows a similar crystallisation pattern, though with higher absolute entropy values and a less pronounced early-layer lead.

\textbf{The centred metric strengthens these findings.}
Figure~\ref{fig:rawvscentered} compares $R$ and $R_c$ across layers.
In Layer 0, $R_c$ diverges dramatically from $R$ at late timesteps: by $t{=}1$, $R \approx 5.7$ but $R_c \approx 35$.
The content score $C$ develops large row-constant components --- energy that inflates its Frobenius norm but that softmax discards entirely.
The geometric bias $B$ retains high within-row variance throughout.
Under the principled centred metric, $B$'s dominance in Layer~0 is approximately $6\times$ greater than the raw metric suggests during refinement.

Layer 6 shows a contrasting pattern: $R/R_c$ rises from 0.6 to 1.8 at late timesteps, indicating that here the raw metric slightly \emph{overestimates} $B$'s contribution.
Layer 11 remains stable at $R/R_c \approx 0.7$.
This layer-dependent centering profile reveals that the geometry--content interplay is qualitatively different at each depth.

\textbf{Sequence separation modulates the balance dynamically.}
Figure~\ref{fig:seqsep} decomposes $R$ by residue separation in Layer~0.
In the 60M model, at $t{=}0$, long-range pairs show the highest dominance ($R_\text{long} = 8.24$), consistent with the intuition that geometry is most needed where sequence proximity provides no structural signal.
After $t \approx 0.2$, the ordering \emph{reverses}: local contacts show $R \approx 8.5$ at $t{=}0.9$, while long-range contacts drop to $R \approx 3.5$.

The 200M model shows a qualitatively different pattern: all sequence-separation categories start near-balanced ($R \approx 0.65$--$0.75$), rise together to peak around $t{=}0.6$ ($R \approx 1.5$--$2.2$), and the separation-dependent ordering is much weaker throughout.
This suggests that while the 60M model relies heavily on $B$ for sequence-separation-specific roles, the larger model distributes this computation more evenly between $B$ and $C$.

\subsection{Functional: Contact Prediction}

Figure~\ref{fig:contact} evaluates the functional utility of each attention component.
In the 60M model, $\mathrm{softmax}(B)$ consistently outperforms $\mathrm{softmax}(C)$: at $t{=}1$, Precision@$L/5$ reaches $0.26 \pm 0.06$ for bias-only versus $0.05 \pm 0.02$ for content-only --- a $5\times$ gap.
Content-only precision starts near random ($\sim$0.002) and grows only modestly.

\textbf{This gap replicates across all three models.}
Both 200M variants show nearly identical contact precision: bias-only reaches $\sim$0.26 versus content-only $\sim$0.05, regardless of whether triangle updates are present.
Despite dramatically lower logit dominance ratios ($R \approx 0.6$--$0.7$ vs.\ 8 in L0), the \emph{functional} superiority of $B$ for contact prediction is preserved across all scales and architectures.
This dissociation between Frobenius norm magnitude and functional utility underscores why multiple lenses are necessary.

Full attention ($0.23 \pm 0.06$) slightly underperforms bias-only in both models, suggesting that $C$ occasionally disagrees with $B$, encoding complementary (non-contact) relationships.
This distinguishes Proteina from static protein language models~\citep{rao2020transformer}, where attention-based contact prediction achieves high precision from fixed, pre-trained representations.
In Proteina, contact prediction is a \emph{dynamic} process, with the geometric bias carrying most of the structural signal throughout.

\subsection{Causal: Bias Ablation}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} presents structural quality under six ablation conditions for both model sizes.
Full removal of $B$ is catastrophic in both models: structures collapse to $R_g \approx 0.12$--$0.14$\,nm with thousands of steric clashes.

The critical finding is the \textbf{temporal asymmetry}, which \textbf{replicates identically across all three models}.
In every case, \emph{early-only B} (active for $t \leq 0.5$, then removed) produces results \emph{indistinguishable from full ablation} --- collapsed structures with thousands of steric clashes.
Conversely, \emph{late-only B} (absent for $t \leq 0.5$, then activated) nearly preserves structural quality ($R_g \geq 1.1$\,nm, $\leq 4$ clashes).

This replication across three architecturally distinct models is striking.
The 60M model shows $R \approx 8$ in Layer~0 at early timesteps; the 200M no-tri model shows $R \approx 0.6$; the 200M tri model shows $R \approx 0.7$.
The descriptive lens would suggest fundamentally different roles for $B$ across these models, yet all three exhibit the identical causal pattern: $B$ is dispensable early and essential late.

\textbf{Triangle updates reshape the layer-specific ablation profile.}
Layer-specific ablations reveal a qualitative difference introduced by triangle multiplicative updates (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}).
In the models without triangle updates, Layer-0 ablation is the most damaging single-layer intervention ($R_g = 0.81$\,nm for 60M, $0.91$\,nm for 200M no-tri), while deep-layer ablation causes intermediate damage.
The 200M tri model inverts this pattern: Layer-0 ablation is \emph{nearly harmless} ($R_g = 1.24$\,nm, 1 clash), while deep-layer ablation is \emph{catastrophic} ($R_g = 0.05$\,nm, 4752 clashes --- worse than full ablation).
This suggests that triangle updates, which iteratively refine the pair representation in deep layers, create a strong dependence on the bias in exactly those layers where the updates operate.
The pair representation becomes the primary information channel in deep layers, making $B$'s contribution there indispensable.

\subsection{Supporting Evidence: Structure Lens}

Figure~\ref{fig:structurelens} shows the result of applying the model's coordinate decoder to intermediate layer representations.
Rather than progressive refinement, we observe that middle layers (L5--7) create representations that are \emph{furthest} from the final output structure (RMSD $\approx 5$--$8$\,nm), while Layer~0 representations are closer ($\approx 1$--$2$\,nm).
This suggests the network warps representations through an internal space that is not aligned with coordinate space --- the middle layers perform significant transformation before the final layers map back to 3D coordinates.
The rightmost panel shows that contact similarity to the final structure (Jaccard index) is near zero until the last 1--2 layers, concentrating structural specificity at the network's output.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Our multi-scale, multi-lens analysis reveals that the geometric bias plays a causally essential but descriptively variable role across model scales.

\textbf{Causal invariance despite descriptive divergence.}
The most striking finding is the complete replication of the temporal ablation pattern across all three architecturally distinct models.
The 60M model's Layer~0 shows $R \approx 8$ (strongly geometry-dominated); both 200M models show $R \approx 0.6$--$0.7$ (near-balanced).
Yet all three exhibit the identical temporal asymmetry: $B$ is dispensable during bootstrapping and essential during refinement, with early-only ablation indistinguishable from full ablation.
This dissociation is important methodologically: the descriptive metric alone would have suggested fundamentally different roles for $B$ across scales, while the causal evidence reveals the same underlying dependence.

\textbf{Triangle updates reshape where --- but not when --- B matters.}
The 200M tri model introduces a second geometric inductive bias: triangle multiplicative updates that iteratively refine the pair representation in deep layers.
This does not change the \emph{temporal} ablation pattern (early-only $\equiv$ full; late-only $\approx$ baseline), but it dramatically alters the \emph{spatial} (layer-specific) profile.
Without triangle updates, Layer-0 ablation is most damaging; with triangle updates, Layer-0 ablation is nearly harmless while deep-layer ablation is catastrophic.
This suggests that triangle updates create a deep-layer information bottleneck through the pair representation, making $B$'s contribution in those layers indispensable --- while simultaneously making earlier layers more robust to missing geometric signal.

\textbf{A three-phase process.}
Both models exhibit a structured generation trajectory: (1)~\emph{bootstrapping} ($t \approx 0$--$0.3$), where attention is diffuse and $B$ is not yet causally essential; (2)~\emph{progressive refinement} ($t \approx 0.3$--$0.8$), where entropy crystallises in a wave from early to late layers; and (3)~\emph{contact commitment} ($t \approx 0.8$--$1.0$), where attention sharpens and $B$ becomes indispensable.

\textbf{Implications for geometric deep learning.}
Our findings provide evidence that geometric inductive biases are not redundant at inference, even in models where their descriptive footprint is small.
The content score $C$ does not learn to replicate $B$'s discriminative signal at either scale --- in the 60M model, $C$'s energy concentrates in softmax-invariant row-constant components; in the 200M model, despite higher $C$ magnitude, $C$-only attention remains equally poor at contact prediction.
For architecture design, this argues that soft geometric priors (additive biases) can be effective alternatives to hard constraints (equivariant layers), provided they are present throughout the generative process --- particularly during refinement.
The scale dependence of the descriptive balance also suggests that conclusions about geometric bias importance drawn from magnitude-based metrics alone may not generalise across model scales.

\textbf{Limitations.}
Spatial alignment is computed against retrospective ground truth (the model's own output), which may overestimate alignment for poorly generated structures.
The Frobenius norm ratio, even after row-centering, does not capture the cross-term interaction $\langle \tilde{B}, \tilde{C} \rangle$ between the two components; a full variance decomposition would further clarify whether $B$ and $C$ reinforce or oppose each other.
Extending this analysis to conditional generation would test whether the causal invariance holds when ground-truth structure is available.

% ============================================================
\section{Conclusion}

We have characterised how Proteina's geometric pair bias and learned content score interact during flow-based protein structure generation, using three complementary analytical lenses across three model variants (60M, 200M without triangle updates, 200M with triangle updates).
The descriptive analysis reveals a scale-dependent geometry--content balance: Layer~0 of the 60M model shows $8\times$ bias dominance, while both 200M models are near-balanced.
Yet the functional analysis shows identical $5\times$ superiority of bias-only contact prediction across all models, and causal ablation reveals the same temporal asymmetry --- $B$ dispensable early, essential late --- replicating across all three architectures.
Triangle multiplicative updates do not change \emph{when} $B$ matters but dramatically alter \emph{where}: they shift $B$'s critical contribution from early to deep layers, creating a pair-representation bottleneck that makes deep-layer bias indispensable.
More broadly, our results show that soft geometric priors play functionally irreplaceable roles in flow-based generation, even in models where their descriptive footprint is small, and that multi-lens interpretability is essential for understanding these roles in nonlinear systems.
